The basics

Regression linealso: fitted line, OLS line

The straight line that best summarizes how Y changes with X. “Best” means it makes the squared vertical distances from the points to the line as small as possible.

On Tab 1, the solid blue line.

Slope (B₁)

How much Y is predicted to change when X goes up by 1.

A slope of 2 means each extra year since the PhD goes with about 2 more publications.

Predicted value (Ŷ)

The Y value the regression line gives for a case's X value: the height of the line above that case.

Residual

Actual Y minus predicted Y: how far a point sits above (+) or below (−) the line.

R²

The share of the variation in Y that the predictors explain, from 0 (none) to 1 (all).

Standard error (SE)

How much an estimate such as B₁ would bounce around if you repeated the study with new samples. A smaller SE means a more precise estimate.

Confidence interval (95% CI)

A range of plausible values for the true effect, roughly the estimate ± 2 standard errors. If the interval includes 0, the effect is usually not statistically significant.

Unusual cases (Tab 1)

Outlier

A case that is very different from the rest, or that has an unreasonably large effect on the regression results. It might be a real but rare case, or a mistake such as a typo.

Leveragestatistic: centered leverage, h*

How unusual a case's X value is, meaning how far it is from the average X. Y doesn't matter at all. High leverage gives a case the potential to tilt the line, like sitting at the end of a seesaw.

Rule of thumb here: h* above .20 (3k/n for small samples).

Discrepancystatistic: studentized deleted residual, SDR

How far a case's Y value is from what the regression predicts for it. To measure this fairly, the line is fit without the case first (that's the “deleted” part), because a case pulls the line toward itself. The distance is then put on a standard scale, like a z-score (the “studentized” part).

Rule of thumb here: beyond ±3.

Influencestatistic: DFFITS

How much the regression results change when a case is deleted. A case usually needs both high leverage and a large discrepancy to be influential.

Rule of thumb here: beyond ±1 for small samples.

Rule of thumb / cutoff

A conventional threshold for flagging a case to look at more closely. It is not a test, and crossing it doesn't automatically mean the case should be deleted.

Overlapping predictors (Tab 2)

Collinearityalso: multicollinearity

When predictors are strongly related to each other, so they carry overlapping information. The regression then has a hard time telling which predictor deserves the credit.

R²j

How much of one predictor can be explained by the other predictors. With just two predictors, it equals their correlation squared (r²).

Tolerance

The share of a predictor that is unique, not overlapping with the other predictors: 1 − R²j.

Rule of thumb: .10 or less is serious.

Variance inflation factor (VIF)

How many times larger the variance of a slope is because of the overlap: 1 / tolerance. Its square root, √VIF, is how many times larger the standard error is.

Rules of thumb: above 5 is a concern, above 10 is serious.

Tab 1: Years since PhD and publications

These data are simulated. They are not real faculty. The 15 cases were generated to look like a small study of faculty productivity: Years since PhD runs from 2 to 18, and publications were created from

Pubs = 4 + 2.0 × Years + random noise (SD = 7), rounded

The starting data were chosen to be “clean”, with no unusual cases. With the original data, the slope is about 2 publications per year (B₁ ≈ 1.99) and R² ≈ .56.

The slide 10 example moves case 15 to 32 years and 6 publications. That could be an emeritus professor who stopped publishing, or a typo. This one change flattens the slope to about 0.07 and drops R² to about .002.

Tab 2: two overlapping predictors

Also simulated. The numbers on Tab 2 are not from one random sample. They are what you would expect for a study of n = 100 students, where both predictors are standardized, the true effect of X1 is B₁ = 0.40, and the outcome's leftover (residual) SD is 1. With only two predictors, X2 explains r² of X1, so

Tolerance = 1 − r²,   VIF = 1 / (1 − r²),   SE(B₁) ≈ 1 / √((n − 1)(1 − r²)),   95% CI ≈ B₁ ± 1.98 × SE(B₁)

Rules of thumb from the notes: VIF > 5 is a concern and VIF > 10 is serious (tolerance ≤ .10).
